Integration by Parts

Integration by Parts

Class Session Goals:

  • Recognize when to use integration by parts.
  • Use the integration-by-parts formula to solve integration problems.
  • Use the integration-by-parts formula for definite integrals.

Recall the Product Rule

To take the derivative of the product of two functions, we uses the product rule.

\[(f(x)g(x))'=f'(x)g(x)+g'(x)f(x)\]

Integration by parts is a technique based on the product rule for differentiation that allows us to exchange one integral for another.

Derivation of Integration by Parts

Integrate both sidesof the product rule

\[\int (f(x)g(x))'\,dx=\int \left(g(x)f'(x)+f(x)g'(x)\right)\,dx.\]

This gives us

\[f(x)g(x)=\int g(x)f'(x)\,dx+\int f(x)g'(x)\,dx.\]

Theorem 3.1

Integration by Parts

Let \(u=f(x)\) and \(v=g(x)\) be functions with continuous derivatives. Then, the integration-by-parts formula for the integral involving these two functions is:

\[\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du.\]

Theorem 3.2

Integration by Parts for Definite Integrals

Let \(u=f(x)\) and \(v=g(x)\) be functions with continuous derivatives on \([a,b]\). Then

\[\int_a^b u\,dv=uv\bigg|_a^b-\int_a^b v\,du.\]

Example 1

Use integration by parts with \(u=x\) and \(dv=\sin x\,dx\) to evaluate \(\int x\sin x\,dx\).

\(u=x\), so \(du=1\,dx\).

\(dv=\sin x\,dx\), so \(v=\int \sin x\,dx=-\cos x\).

\[\int x\sin x\,dx=-x\cos x+\sin x+C.\]

Example 2

Evaluate \(\int \frac{\ln x}{x^3}\,dx\).

\(u=\ln x\) and \(dv=x^{-3}\,dx\).

\(du=\frac{1}{x}\,dx\) and \(v=\int x^{-3}\,dx=-\frac{1}{2}x^{-2}\).

\[\int \frac{\ln x}{x^3}\,dx=-\frac{1}{2x^2}\ln x-\frac{1}{4x^2}+C.\]

LIATE

The acronym LIATE can often help to take some of the guesswork out of our choices.

  • Logarithmic Functions

The type of function in the integral that appears first in the list should be our first choice of \(u\).

Using parts more than once

Evaluate \(\int x^2e^{3x}\,dx\).

\(u=x^2\) and \(dv=e^{3x}\,dx\).

\(du=2x\,dx\) and \(v=\frac{1}{3}e^{3x}\).

\[\int x^2e^{3x}\,dx=\frac{1}{3}x^2e^{3x}-\frac{2}{9}xe^{3x}+\frac{2}{27}e^{3x}+C.\]

Activity

For each problem below first identify \(u\) and \(dv\), then calculate \(du\) and \(v\), and then rewrite the integral using Theorem 3.1.

  1. \(\int x^3e^{2x}\,dx\)
  2. \(\int x^3\ln(x)\,dx\)
  3. \(\int y^3\cos y\,dy\)
  4. \(\int x^2\arctan x\,dx\)
  5. \(\int e^{3x}\sin(2x)\,dx\)

Answers to activity 1

  1. \(u=x^3\), \(dv=e^{2x}\,dx\), \(du=3x^2\,dx\), \(v=\frac{1}{2}e^{2x}\)

    \(\int x^3e^{2x}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}x^3e^{2x}-\int \frac{3}{2}x^2e^{2x}\,dx\)

  2. \(u=\ln x\), \(dv=x^3\,dx\), \(du=\frac{1}{x}\,dx\), \(v=\frac{x^4}{4}\)

    \(\int x^3\ln(x)\,dx=\frac{x^4}{4}\ln x-\int \frac{x^3}{4}\,dx\)

  3. \(u=y^3\), \(dv=\cos y\,dy\), \(du=3y^2\,dy\), \(v=\sin y\)

    \(\int y^3\cos y\,dy=y^3\sin y-\int 3y^2\sin y\,dy\)

Answers to activity 2

  1. \(u=\arctan x\), \(dv=x^2\,dx\), \(du=\frac{1}{x^2+1}\,dx\), \(v=\frac{x^3}{3}\)

    \(\int x^2\arctan x\,dx=\frac{x^3}{3}\arctan x-\int \frac{x^3}{3(x^2+1)}\,dx\)

  2. \(u=\sin(2x)\), \(dv=e^{3x}\,dx\), \(du=2\cos(2x)\,dx\), \(v=\frac{1}{3}e^{3x}\)

    \(\int e^{3x}\sin(2x)\,dx=\frac{1}{3}e^{3x}\sin(2x)-\int \frac{2}{3}e^{3x}\cos(2x)\,dx\)

Ancient Egyptian Fractions

Ancient Egyptians wrote fractions as sums of unit fractions, fractions with numerator \(1\).

Example: rewrite \(\frac{5}{6}\) as a sum of unit fractions.

Choose the largest unit fraction less than \(\frac{5}{6}\) which is \(\frac{1}{2}\)

Subtract:\(\frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{5}{6}-\frac{3}{6}=\frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3}\)

So, \[\frac{5}{6}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}\]

This process is an exmple of the greedy algorithm.

Ancient Egyptian Fractions: Another Example

Rewrite \(\frac{11}{12}\) as a sum of unit fractions.

Choose the largest unit fraction less than \(\frac{11}{12}\): \(\frac{1}{2}\)

Subtract \(\frac{11}{12}-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{5}{12}\)

Next choose \(\frac{1}{3}\) and subtract \(\frac{5}{12}-\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1}{12}\) to get:

\[\frac{11}{12}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{12}\]

Finding a Common Denominator

Recall how we add fractions:

\[\frac{2}{x}+\frac{3}{x+1} =\frac{2(x+1)+3x}{x(x+1)} =\frac{5x+2}{x(x+1)}\]

Reversing the process

Partial fraction decomposition reverses this process.

Starting with \(\frac{5x+2}{x(x+1)}\) we try to rewrite it as

\[\frac{A}{x}+\frac{B}{x+1}.\]

Finding partial fractions means determining the values of \(A\) and \(B\) so that the two expressions are equal.

Example of Partial Fractions 1

Decompose \(\frac{3x+1}{x^2-x-2}.\)

\[\frac{3x+1}{(x-2)(x+1)} =\frac{A}{x-2}+\frac{B}{x+1}.\]

Multiply by the common denominator:

\[3x+1=A(x+1)+B(x-2).\]

\[\frac{3x+1}{(x-2)(x+1)} =\frac{7}{3(x-2)}+\frac{2}{3(x+1)}.\]

Using Example 1 to integrate

Now evaluate \(\int \frac{3x+1}{x^2-x-2}\,dx.\)

Using the partial fraction decomposition,

\[\int\left( \frac{7}{3(x-2)} +\frac{2}{3(x+1)} \right)\,dx.\]

\[\frac{7}{3}\ln|x-2| +\frac{2}{3}\ln|x+1|+C.\]

Table of Antiderivatives: Trig

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \sec^2 x\,dx &= \tan x+C \\ \int \csc^2 x\,dx &= -\cot x+C \\ \int \sec x\tan x\,dx &= \sec x+C \\ \int \csc x\cot x\,dx &= -\csc x+C \end{aligned} \]

Table of Antiderivatives: Trig

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \tan x\,dx &= \ln|\sec x|+C \\ \int \cot x\,dx &= \ln|\sin x|+C \\ \int \sec x\,dx &= \ln|\sec x+\tan x|+C \\ \int \csc x\,dx &= -\ln|\csc x+\cot x|+C \end{aligned} \]

Table of Antiderivatives: Other

\[ \begin{aligned} \int a^x\,dx &= \frac{a^x}{\ln a}+C,\quad a>0,\ a\neq1 \\ \int \log_a x\,dx &= x\log_a x-\frac{x}{\ln a}+C,\quad a>0,\ a\neq1 \\ \int \frac{1}{a^2+x^2}\,dx &= \frac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)+C \end{aligned} \]

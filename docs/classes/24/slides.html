Power Series and Functions

Class Session Goals:

  • Identify a power series and provide examples of them.
  • Determine the radius of convergence and interval of convergence of a power series.
  • Use a power series to represent a function.

Motivation

Power Series are an alternative way to represent functions

  • Power series are infinite polynomials
  • Polynomials are easy to evaluate, differentiate, integrate, and approximate.
  • Power series allow us to represent common functions and define new functions.
  • We can use polynomial approximations to estimate values of functions.

Definition of a Power Series 1

A series of the form

\(\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_nx^n =c_0+c_1x+c_2x^2+\cdots\)

is a power series centered at \(x=0\).

\(x\) is a variable, and the coefficients \(c_n\) are functions of \(n\) that do not depend on \(x\).

The value of this series depends on \(x\) so it is a function of \(x\).

Definition of a Power Series 2

A series of the form

\(\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n =c_0+c_1(x-a)+c_2(x-a)^2+\cdots\)

is a power series centered at \(x=a\).

\(x\) and \(c_n\) are as before, and \(a\) is a constant.

One way to think of \(a\) as the location where we examining a funciton.

Example 1: Exponential Function

Consider the power series

\(\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^n}{n!}\)

Expanding the series gives

\(\displaystyle 1+x+\frac{x^2}{2!}+\frac{x^3}{3!}+\cdots\)

We can define a function \(f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^n}{n!}\). If \(x=0\), then \(f(0)=1\).

Example 2: Geometeric Series

Consider the geometric series

\(\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^n =1+x+x^2+x^3+\cdots\)

This series converges if \(|x|<1\) and if it converges the sum is \(\frac{1}{1-x}\)

So we have:

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{1-x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^n\)

for \(|x|<1\).

Here the center is \(a=0\) and the coefficients are \(c_n=1\) for all \(n\).

Convergence of Power Series

Consider the power series

\(\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n\)

  • A power series may converge for some values of \(x\) and diverge for others.
  • At \(x=a\), the series becomes \(c_0\) and power series always converges at its center.

The possible sets of convergence are: only the center \(x=a\), all real numbers, an interval centered at \(x=a\), possibly including one or both endpoints.

Theorem 6.1 Convergence of a Power Series

Consider the power series \(\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n\).

The series satisfies exactly one of the following properties:

  1. The series converges at \(x=a\) and diverges for all \(x\ne a\).

  2. The series converges for all real numbers \(x\).

  3. There exists a real number \(R>0\) such that the series converges if \(|x-a|<R\) and diverges if \(|x-a|>R\).

    At the values \(x\) where \(|x-a|=R\), the series may converge or diverge.

Proof of Theorem 6.1

Key fact: If \(\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_nd^n\) converges for some \(d\ne0\), then \(\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_nx^n\) converges absolutely for \(|x|<|d|\).

Since \(\sum c_nd^n\) converges, \(c_nd^n\to0\).

Thus, for sufficiently large \(n\), \(|c_nd^n|\le1\).

Consequently,

\(\displaystyle |c_nx^n|=|c_nd^n|\left|\frac{x}{d}\right|^n \le\left|\frac{x}{d}\right|^n\)

The geometric series \(\displaystyle \sum\left|\frac{x}{d}\right|^n\) converges whenever \(|x|<|d|\).

By the comparison test, \(\sum c_nx^n\) converges absolutely.

For a general center \(a\), substitute \(y=x-a\).

What Does Theorem 6.1 Tell Us?

If a power series converges at a point, then it converges absolutely at every point closer to its center.

Consider a power series centered at \(a=2\) that converges at \(x=5\).

  • What can we conclude about convergence at \(x=3\)?
  • What can we conclude about convergence at \(x=0\)?
  • What can we conclude about convergence at \(x=6\)?

Definition of Radius of Convergence

Consider the power series \(\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n\).

The interval of convergence is the set of real numbers \(x\) where the series converges.

The radius of convergence is the value \(R\) such that:

  • The series converges for \(|x-a|<R\).
  • The series diverges for \(|x-a|>R\).
  • At \(x=a-R\) and \(x=a+R\), convergence must be checked separately.

Special cases:

  • If the series converges only at \(x=a\), then \(R=0\).
  • If the series converges for all real numbers, then \(R=\infty\).

Representing Functions as Power Series

Summary

  • A power series is an infinite polynomial.
  • Every power series has a radius of convergence.
  • A function can be represented by a power series where the power series converges.

Given a function, how can we construct a power series representation of a function? Where is the power series represenation valid?

Recall: Geometric Series

A geometric series is a series of the form

\(\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}ar^n=a+ar+ar^2+ar^3+\cdots\)

where \(a\) is the first term and \(r\) is the common ratio.

  • If \(|r|<1\), the series converges, and

    \(\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}ar^n=\frac{a}{1-r}\)

  • If \(|r|\geq1\) and \(a\ne0\), the series diverges.

Special case: When \(a=1\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{1-r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^n\)

for \(|r|<1\).

Rational Functions as Power Series

Recall the geometric series formula:

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{1-r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^n =1+r+r^2+r^3+\cdots\)

for \(|r|<1\).

We can use this formula to represent certain rational functions as power series.

Strategy:

  1. Rewrite the function in the form \(\frac{A}{1-r}\).
  2. Identify the geometric ratio \(r\).
  3. Substitute into the geometric series formula.
  4. Solve \(|r|<1\) to determine the interval of convergence.

Example 1

\(\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{1}{1+x^3}\)

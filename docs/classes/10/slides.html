Moments Centers of Mass

Moments and Center of Mass

Class Session Goals:

  • Find the center of mass of objects distributed along a line.
  • Locate the center of mass of a thin plate.
  • Use symmetry to help locate the centroid of a thin plate.

Core Idea of Chapter 2

Integration gives us a way to add up infinitely small pieces.

Definition of the Definite Integral:

\[ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n\to\infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x \]

where \(f(x_i^*)\Delta x\) gives an approximation of a small “piece”.

It can be a small piece of area, volume, mass, or work.

Center of Mass on a Line Formula

Let \(m_1,m_2,\ldots,m_n\) be point masses placed on a number line at points \(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n\), respectively.

The total mass is

\(m=\sum_{i=1}^n m_i\)

The moment of the system with respect to the origin is

\(M=\sum_{i=1}^n m_ix_i\)

The center of mass is

\(\bar{x}=\frac{M}{m}\)

Center of Mass on a Line Example

Suppose four point masses are placed on a number line:

\(m_1=30\) kg at \(x_1=-2\) m

\(m_2=5\) kg at \(x_2=3\) m

\(m_3=10\) kg at \(x_3=6\) m

\(m_4=15\) kg at \(x_4=-3\) m

Find the moment of the system and the center of mass.

Center of Mass on a Line Example

First, calculate the moment:

\(M=\sum_{i=1}^4 m_ix_i\)

\(M=-60+15+60-45=-30\)

The total mass is

\(m=30+5+10+15=60\) kg

Therefore,

\(\bar{x}=\frac{M}{m}=\frac{-30}{60}=-\frac{1}{2}\)

The center of mass is located \(1/2\) m to the left of the origin.

Center of Mass on a Plane Formula

Let \(m_1,m_2,\ldots,m_n\) be point masses located in the \(xy\)-plane at points \((x_1,y_1),(x_2,y_2),\ldots,(x_n,y_n)\).

The moments are

\(M_x=\sum_{i=1}^n m_iy_i\)

and

\(M_y=\sum_{i=1}^n m_ix_i\)

The center of mass is

\(\bar{x}=\frac{M_y}{m}\) and \(\bar{y}=\frac{M_x}{m}\)

Center of Mass on a Plane Example

Suppose three point masses are placed in the \(xy\)-plane:

\(m_1=2\) kg at \((-1,3)\)

\(m_2=6\) kg at \((1,1)\)

\(m_3=4\) kg at \((2,-2)\)

The total mass is

\(m=2+6+4=12\) kg

Center of Mass on a Plane Example

Find the moments:

\(M_y=\sum_{i=1}^3 m_ix_i=-2+6+8=12\)

\(M_x=\sum_{i=1}^3 m_iy_i=6+6-8=4\)

Then

\(\bar{x}=\frac{M_y}{m}=\frac{12}{12}=1\)

\(\bar{y}=\frac{M_x}{m}=\frac{4}{12}=\frac{1}{3}\)

The center of mass is \((1,1/3)\), in meters.

Generalizing Moment Formula 1

Suppose a lamina is bounded above by \(f(x)\), below by the \(x\)-axis, and on the left and right by \(x=a\) and \(x=b\).

Let \(\rho\) denote the density of the associated lamina.

The mass of the lamina is

\(m=\rho\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\)

Generalizing Moment Formula 2

A representative rectangle has height \(f(x_i^*)\).

Its center of mass is

\((x_i^*,f(x_i^*)/2)\)

The moment with respect to the \(x\)-axis is

\(M_x=\rho\int_a^b \frac{[f(x)]^2}{2}\,dx\)

Generalizing Moment Formula 3

The moment with respect to the \(y\)-axis is

\(M_y=\rho\int_a^b xf(x)\,dx\)

The coordinates of the center of mass are

\(\bar{x}=\frac{M_y}{m}\) and \(\bar{y}=\frac{M_x}{m}\)

Since \(\rho\) cancels out, the centroid depends only on the shape of the region.

Example 1

Let \(R\) be the region bounded above by \(f(x)=\sqrt{x}\) and below by the \(x\)-axis over \([0,4]\).

Find the centroid of the region.

Assume \(\rho=1\).

\(m=\int_0^4 \sqrt{x}\,dx=\left.\frac{2}{3}x^{3/2}\right|_0^4=\frac{16}{3}\)

Example 1

Compute the moments:

\(M_x=\int_0^4 \frac{x}{2}\,dx=\left.\frac{1}{4}x^2\right|_0^4=4\)

\(M_y=\int_0^4 x\sqrt{x}\,dx=\int_0^4 x^{3/2}\,dx\)

\(M_y=\left.\frac{2}{5}x^{5/2}\right|_0^4=\frac{64}{5}\)

Thus,

\(\bar{x}=\frac{64/5}{16/3}=\frac{12}{5}\)

\(\bar{y}=\frac{4}{16/3}=\frac{3}{4}\)

The centroid is \((12/5,3/4)\).

Example 2

Let \(R\) be the region bounded above by \(f(x)=1-x^2\) and below by \(g(x)=x-1\).

The graphs intersect at \((-2,-3)\) and \((1,0)\), so integrate from \(-2\) to \(1\).

\(m=\int_{-2}^{1}[1-x^2-(x-1)]\,dx\)

\(m=\int_{-2}^{1}(2-x^2-x)\,dx=\frac{9}{2}\)

Example 2

For a region between two functions,

\(M_x=\int_a^b \frac{1}{2}\left([f(x)]^2-[g(x)]^2\right)\,dx\)

\(M_y=\int_a^b x[f(x)-g(x)]\,dx\)

For this region,

\(M_x=-\frac{27}{10}\) and \(M_y=-\frac{9}{4}\)

Therefore,

\(\bar{x}=\frac{M_y}{m}=-\frac{1}{2}\)

\(\bar{y}=\frac{M_x}{m}=-\frac{3}{5}\)

The centroid is \((-1/2,-3/5)\).

Example 3

Let \(R\) be the region bounded above by \(f(x)=4-x^2\) and below by the \(x\)-axis.

The region is symmetric with respect to the \(y\)-axis.

Therefore, the \(x\)-coordinate of the centroid is zero.

\(m=\int_{-2}^{2}(4-x^2)\,dx=\frac{32}{3}\)

Example 3

We only need \(M_x\):

\(M_x=\int_{-2}^{2}\frac{(4-x^2)^2}{2}\,dx\)

\(M_x=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-2}^{2}(16-8x^2+x^4)\,dx\)

\(M_x=\frac{256}{15}\)

Then

\(\bar{y}=\frac{M_x}{m}=\frac{256}{15}\cdot\frac{3}{32}=\frac{8}{5}\)

The centroid is \((0,8/5)\).

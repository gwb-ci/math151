Core Idea of Chapter 2

Integration gives us a way to add up infinitely small slices

Defintion of the Definite Integral: \[ \boxed{ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x } \]

Volume and Slicing

Suppose a solid extends from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\).

If the area of the cross-section perpendicular to the \(x\)-axis is \(A(x)\), then

\[ \boxed{ V=\int_a^b A(x)\,dx } \]

Other ways to slice

We started with circular slices because that is a good starting point. We have this linear slices which lines up with what we think of slices in everyday life. But math is about generalizing an idea, so we ask the question “Can we do this for other types of slices?”

  • circular slices
  • cylindrical slices, think cookie cutters
  • elliptical slices
  • spherical slices, think onion peels

Cylindrical Shells Method

With the method of cylindrical shells, we integrate along the coordinate axis perpendicular to the axis of revolution.

Shell volume:

\(V_{\text{shell}}\approx 2\pi(\text{radius})(\text{height})(\text{thickness})\)

For rotation about the \(y\)-axis:

\(V=\int_a^b 2\pi x f(x)\,dx\)

Rule: The Method of Cylindrical Shells

Let \(f(x)\) be continuous and nonnegative.

Define \(R\) as the region bounded above by the graph of \(f(x)\), below by the \(x\)-axis, on the left by the line \(x=a\), and on the right by the line \(x=b\).

The volume of the solid of revolution formed by revolving \(R\) around the \(y\)-axis is

\(V=\int_a^b2\pi x f(x)\,dx\)

Cylindrical Shells Example 1

Region: under \(f(x)=\frac{1}{x}\) over \([1,3]\)

Rotate around the \(y\)-axis.

Function Plot

\(V=\int_1^3\left(2\pi x\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\right)\,dx\)

\(V=\int_1^3 2\pi\,dx=2\pi x\big|_1^3=4\pi\)

Cylindrical Shells Example 2

Region: under \(f(x)=2x-x^2\) and above the x-axis.

Rotate around the \(y\)-axis.

Function Plot

\(V=\int_0^2\left(2\pi x(2x-x^2)\right)\,dx\)

\(V=2\pi\int_0^2(2x^2-x^3)\,dx\)

\(V=2\pi\left[\frac{2x^3}{3}-\frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^2=\frac{8\pi}{3}\)

Cylindrical Shells Example 3

Let \(Q\) be bounded on the right by \(g(y)=2\sqrt{y}\) and on the left by the \(y\)-axis for \(y\in[0,4]\).

Rotate around the \(x\)-axis.

Function Plot

\(V=\int_0^4\left(2\pi y(2\sqrt{y})\right)\,dy\)

\(V=4\pi\int_0^4 y^{3/2}\,dy =4\pi\left[\frac{2y^{5/2}}{5}\right]_0^4 =\frac{256\pi}{5}\)

Rotation around other axes

We can rotate areas around other lines too, we are not just restriced to the x-axis of the y-axis. When we do the main thing that changes is the radius. We aske the question, “What is the radius of the circles or cylinders I will be using?” and adjust the formulas.

Washer Method with a Different Axis of Rotation

For a region revolved around a horizontal line \(y=c\):

\(V=\pi\int_a^b\left([R(x)]^2-[r(x)]^2\right)\,dx\)

where

\(R(x)=\) distance from \(y=c\) to the outer curve

\(r(x)=\) distance from \(y=c\) to the inner curve

Washer Method with a Different Axis of Rotation

If the region is bounded above by \(f(x)\) and below by \(g(x)\), then the radii depend on the location of the axis of rotation.

For rotation about \(y=c\) below the region:

\(R(x)=f(x)-c,\qquad r(x)=g(x)-c\)

For rotation about \(y=c\) above the region:

\(R(x)=c-g(x),\qquad r(x)=c-f(x)\)

Understanding the idea here is far more important than memorizing the formulas.

Revolution About a Different Axis: Shell Method

Region: under \(f(x)=x\) over \([1,2]\)

Rotate around the line \(x=-1\).

Function Plot

Radius of a shell: \(x+1\)

\(V=\int_1^2\left(2\pi(x+1)f(x)\right)\,dx\)

\(V=2\pi\int_1^2(x^2+x)\,dx =2\pi\left[\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_1^2 =\frac{23\pi}{3}\)

How to choose which Volume Method to Use

It often comes down to a choice of which integral is easiest to evaluate.

Disk method: slices perpendicular to the axis; no cavity.

\(V=\int_a^b \pi[f(x)]^2\,dx\)

Washer method: slices perpendicular to the axis; cavity or two boundaries.

\(V=\int_a^b \pi\left([f(x)]^2-[g(x)]^2\right)\,dx\)

Cylindrical Shell method: slices parallel to the axis.

\(V=\int 2\pi x f(x) \,dx\)

Table of Antiderivatives: Trig

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \tan x\,dx &= \ln|\sec x|+C \\ \int \cot x\,dx &= \ln|\sin x|+C \\ \int \sec x\,dx &= \ln|\sec x+\tan x|+C \\ \int \csc x\,dx &= -\ln|\csc x+\cot x|+C \end{aligned} \]

\[ \begin{aligned} \int a^x\,dx &= \frac{a^x}{\ln a}+C,\quad a>0,\ a\neq1 \\ \int \log_a x\,dx &= x\log_a x-\frac{x}{\ln a}+C,\quad a>0,\ a\neq1 \\ \int \frac{1}{a^2+x^2}\,dx &= \frac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)+C \end{aligned} \]

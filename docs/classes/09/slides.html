Mass, Density, Force, and Work

Surface Area and Weight

Class Session Goals:

Definition of the Definite Integral:

\[ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n\to\infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x \]

where \(f(x_i^*)\Delta x\) gives an approximation of a small “piece”.

It can be a small piece of area, volume, mass, or work.

Density of Circular Objects

Now consider a thin two-dimensional disk of radius \(r\).

Density here is measured as mass per unit area. Suppose the density depends only on the distance from the center.

This is called radial density, \(\rho(x)\). Then \(\text{mass}=\rho \cdot \text{area}\).

Because all points the same distance \(x\) from the center have the same density, we divide the disk into thin circular washers.

Density Approximation of a Circular Object

Consider a washer between radii \(x_{i-1}\) and \(x_i\).

Its area is

\[ \begin{aligned} A_i &=\pi x_i^2-\pi x_{i-1}^2 \end{aligned} \]

Using \(x_i^*\approx\dfrac{x_i+x_{i-1}}{2}\), we get \(A_i\approx2\pi x_i^*\Delta x\).

Therefore the mass of the washer is approximately

\[ m_i \approx 2\pi x_i^*\rho(x_i^*)\Delta x. \]

Approximating Mass

Adding all the washers,

\(\text{mass}\approx \displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^n 2\pi x_i^*\rho(x_i^*)\Delta x\).

Then take the limit

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{mass} &=\lim_{n \rightarrow \infty} \sum_{i=1}^n 2\pi x_i^*\rho(x_i^*)\Delta x \\ &=\int^r_0 2 \pi x \rho(x)\, dx$ \end{aligned} \]

Mass-Density Formula of a Circular Object

Let \(\rho(x)\) be an integrable function representing the radial density of a disk of radius \(r\).

Then the mass of the disk is

\[ \text{Mass}=\int_0^r2\pi x\rho(x)\,dx \]

Mass Density Example 1

Let \(\rho(x)=\sqrt{x}\) be the radial density of a disk of radius \(4\). Find the mass of this disk.

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{mass} &=\int_0^4 2\pi x\rho(x)\,dx\\ &=\frac{128\pi}{5} \end{aligned} \]

Mass Density Example 2

Let \(\rho(x)=2-|x|\) the density of a thin plate as a function of \(x\). Suppost the shape is the region bounded by \(f(x)= 4-x^2\) and the \(x\)-axis. What is the mass of this plate?

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{mass} &=\int_{-2}^2 (2-|x|) (4-x^2)\,dx\\ &=\frac{40}{3} \end{aligned} \]

Work Done by Force

When a force moves an object, we say the force does work on the object.

For a constant force, work is the product of force and distance: \(W = Fd\).

For a variable force \(F(x)\) moving an object from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\),

\(W_i \approx F(x_i^*)\Delta x\)

\(W \approx \sum_{i=1}^n F(x_i^*)\Delta x\)

Taking the limit gives

\(W=\int_a^b F(x)\,dx\)

Hooke’s Law and Spring Constant

According to Hooke’s law, the force required to compress or stretch a spring from equilibrium is

\(F(x)=kx\) where \(k\) is the spring constant and \(k>0\).

The work done to stretch or compress the spring from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\) is

\(W=\int_a^b kx\,dx\)

Spring Example 1

Suppose it takes a force of \(10\) N in the negative direction to compress a spring \(0.2\) m from equilibrium. How much work is done to stretch the spring \(0.5\) m from equilibrium?

First find \(k\). Then find work.

\(k=50\)

\(W=\frac{25}{4}=6.25\) J.

Spring Example 2

A \(1\) spring requires \(10\) J to stretch the spring to \(1.1\) m. How much work would it take to stretch the sprint from \(1\) to \(1.2\) m?

Find \(k\), then find work.

\(W=40J\)

Work to Pump Out a Circular Tank

Assume a cylindrical tank of radius \(4\) m and height \(10\) m is filled to a depth of \(8\) m.

A representative layer has volume \(V=\pi(4)^2\Delta x=16\pi\Delta x\). The mass of water is about \(1 \text{kg}/\text{cm}^3\)

The force needed to lift each layer is \(F=9800\cdot16\pi\Delta x=156{,}800\pi\Delta x\)

The distance the layer must be lifted is approximately \(x_i^*\).

So the work for one slice is \(W_i\approx156{,}800\pi x_i^*\Delta x\)

Pumping Work Example

Adding the work for each layer gives

\(W\approx\sum_{i=1}^n156{,}800\pi x_i^*\Delta x\)

Taking the limit gives

\(W=156{,}800\pi\int_2^{10}x\,dx\)

\(W=156{,}800\pi\left.\frac{x^2}{2}\right|_2^{10}\)

\(W=7{,}526{,}400\pi\approx23{,}644{,}883\)

The work required to empty the tank is approximately \(23{,}650{,}000\) J.

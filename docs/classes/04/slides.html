Area and Volume

Class Session Goals:

  • Find the area of a region between two curves.
  • Find the volume of a solid using slicing.

Core Idea of Chapter 2

Integration gives us a way to add up infinitely small slices

Defintion of the Definite Integral: \[ \boxed{ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x } \]

Two ways to think about the area between Curves

Assume that \(f(x) \geq g(x)\) on \([a,b]\). \[ \text{Area}=\int_a^b \text{top}\,dx-\int_a^b \text{bottom}\,dx \]

or

\[ \text{Area}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n} [f(x_i^*)-g(x_i^*)]\,\Delta x \]

Theorem 2.1 Area Between Curves

Let \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\) be continuous functions such that

\[ f(x)\geq g(x) \]

over an interval \([a,b]\).

If \(R\) is the region bounded above by \(f(x)\), below by \(g(x)\), and on the left and right by \(x=a\) and \(x=b\), then

\[ \boxed{ A=\int_a^b [f(x)-g(x)]\,dx } \]

Theorem 2.2 Area Between Curves that Cross

If the graphs cross, the identity of the top function and bottom function may change.

The area between two continuous functions over \([a,b]\) is

\[ A=\int_a^b |f(x)-g(x)|\,dx. \]

Note: This is different from the value of the definite integral \(\int_a^b [f(x)-g(x)]\,dx\).

Managing the Crossings

In practice:

  1. Find the points where \(f(x)=g(x)\).

  2. Use the intersection points to divide the interval.

  3. On each interval, identify the top and bottom functions.

  4. Add the areas of the subregions.

Changing which slices you use

Sometimes a region is easier to describe using horizontal slices.

Instead of writing \(y=f(x)\) write the boundaries as functions of \(y\):

\(x=u(y) \qquad\text{and}\qquad x=v(y)\).

For a horizontal slice, \(\text{width} = \text{right}-\text{left}\).

Therefore, \(\text{Area} = \int[\text{right}-\text{left}]\,dy\).

The limits of integration are now \(y\)-values.

Definite Integral with respect to y

\[ \boxed{ \int_a^b f(y)\,dy = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n} f(y_i^*)\,\Delta y } \]

Example of Area Between Curves with respect to y

Theorem 2.3 Area between Curves with respect to y

Let \(u(y)\) and \(v(y)\) be continuous functions such that

\[ u(y)\geq v(y) \]

for all \(y\in[c,d]\).

If \(R\) is bounded on the right by \(u(y)\), on the left by \(v(y)\), and above and below by \(y=d\) and \(y=c\), then

\[ \boxed{ A=\int_c^d [u(y)-v(y)]\,dy } \]

Slices of with Area \(A(x)\)

Integration, we can add up infinitely many slices of area to find the volume of a solid.

\[\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n} A(x_i^*)\,\Delta x\]

Volume and Slicing

Suppose a solid extends from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\).

If the area of the cross-section perpendicular to the \(x\)-axis is \(A(x)\), then

\[ \boxed{ V=\int_a^b A(x)\,dx } \]

Volume of a Hut

Consider a circular hut whose cross-section is a circle. The hut is 5 feet tall and at its base the diameter is 10 The radius \(r\) of the circular cross-section varies with the height \(h\) of the hut. The radius is given by \(r(h)=5-\frac{h^2}{5}\). What is the volume of the hut?

Finding Volumes by the Slicing Method

  1. Determine the shape of a cross-section.
  2. Determine a formula for the area of the cross-section.
  3. Integrate the area formula over the appropriate interval.

\[ \text{Volume} = \int \text{cross-sectional area}. \]

Volume of a Solid of Revolution

If a region in a plane is revolved around a line in that plane, the resulting solid is called a solid of revolution.

For example, rotate a region around the \(x\)-axis.

A vertical slice of the region rotates to form a circular cross-section.

The volume is still found using

\[ V=\int_a^b A(x)\,dx, \]

but now \(A(x)\) is the area of a circular cross-section.

Disk Method

Suppose a region lies between

\[ y=f(x) \]

and the \(x\)-axis.

When the region is revolved around the \(x\)-axis, a vertical slice forms a disk.

The radius of the disk is

\[ r=f(x). \]

Since the area of a circle is

\[ A=\pi r^2, \]

the cross-sectional area is

\[ A(x)=\pi[f(x)]^2. \]

Disk Method Formula

Let \(f(x)\) be continuous and nonnegative over \([a,b]\).

If the region under \(f(x)\) is revolved around the \(x\)-axis, then

\[ \boxed{ V=\int_a^b \pi[f(x)]^2\,dx } \]

Equivalently,

\[ \boxed{ V=\pi\int_a^b [f(x)]^2\,dx } \]

For revolution around the \(y\)-axis, with \(x=g(y)\),

\[ \boxed{ V=\int_c^d \pi[g(y)]^2\,dy } \]

Disk Method Example

Find the volume of the solid generated by revolving the region bounded by

\[ f(x)=\sqrt{x}, \]

the \(x\)-axis, \(x=1\), and \(x=4\)

around the \(x\)-axis.

Set up

Radius:

\[ r=f(x)=\sqrt{x} \]

Cross-sectional area:

\[ A(x)=\pi(\sqrt{x})^2 \]

Volume:

\[ V=\int_1^4 \pi(\sqrt{x})^2\,dx \]

Washer Method

A solid of revolution may have a cavity in the middle.

A cross-section is then a washer rather than a disk.

Let

\[ R(x)=\text{outer radius} \]

and

\[ r(x)=\text{inner radius}. \]

The area of a washer is

\[ A(x) = \pi[R(x)]^2-\pi[r(x)]^2. \]

So,

\[ A(x) = \pi\left([R(x)]^2-[r(x)]^2\right). \]

Washer Method Formula

Suppose \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\) are continuous, nonnegative functions with

\[ f(x)\geq g(x) \]

on \([a,b]\).

When the region between the curves is revolved around the \(x\)-axis,

\[ \boxed{ V = \int_a^b \pi\left([f(x)]^2-[g(x)]^2\right)\,dx } \]

Think:

\[ \boxed{ V = \pi\int_a^b \left[ (\text{outer radius})^2 - (\text{inner radius})^2 \right]\,dx } \]

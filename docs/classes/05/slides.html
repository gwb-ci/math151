Volume by Disks and Washers

Calculating Volume using Slicing

Class Session Goals:

  • Find the volume of a solid using slicing.
  • Find the volume of revolution using the disk/washer method.

Core Idea of Chapter 2

Integration gives us a way to add up infinitely small slices

Defintion of the Definite Integral: \[ \boxed{ \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x } \]

Volume and Slicing

Suppose a solid extends from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\).

If the area of the cross-section perpendicular to the \(x\)-axis is \(A(x)\), then

\[ \boxed{ V=\int_a^b A(x)\,dx } \]

Volume of a Lean to Shelter

Consider a triangular lean-to shelter whose cross-section is a rectangle. The shelter is 5 feet tall and at its base is a 5 foot by 8 foot rectangle. The area \(A\) of the rectangular cross-section varies with the height \(h\) of the shelter. \(A(h)=(5-h)\cdot 8\). What is the volume of the shelter?

Finding Volumes by the Slicing Method

  1. Determine the shape of a cross-section.
  2. Determine a formula for the area of the cross-section.
  3. Integrate the area formula over the appropriate interval.

\[ \text{Volume} = \int \text{cross-sectional area}. \]

Volume of a cone

Consider a cone with height \(h\) and base radius \(r\). The radius of a cross-section at height \(y\) is given by \(R(y)=\frac{r}{h}(h-y)\). The volume of the cone is

\[ V=\int_0^h \pi[R(y)]^2\,dy =\int_0^h \pi\left[\frac{r}{h}(h-y)\right]^2\,dy =\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h. \]

Volume of a Solid of Revolution

If a region in a plane is revolved around a line in that plane, the resulting solid is called a solid of revolution.

If we rotate a region around the \(x\)-axis. A vertical slice of the region rotates to form a circular cross-section.

The volume is still found using \(V=\int_a^b A(x)\,dx\).

but now \(A(x)\) is the area of a circular cross-section.

Disk Method

Suppose a region lies between \(y=f(x)\) and the \(x\)-axis.

When the region is revolved around the \(x\)-axis, a vertical slice forms a disk.

The radius of the disk is \(f(x)\) and the area of the corresponding circle is \(A(x)=\pi[f(x)]^2\).

Disk Method Formula

Let \(f(x)\) be continuous and nonnegative over \([a,b]\).

If the region under \(f(x)\) is revolved around the \(x\)-axis, then

\[ \boxed{ V=\int_a^b \pi[f(x)]^2\,dx } \]

Washer Method

A solid of revolution may have a cavity in the middle.

A cross-section is then a washer rather than a disk.

Let \(f(x)=\text{outer radius}\) and \(g(x)=\text{inner radius}\)

The area of a washer is

\[ A(x) = \pi[f(x)]^2-\pi[g(x)]^2 \]

Washer Method Formula

Suppose \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\) are continuous, nonnegative functions with

\[ f(x)\geq g(x) \]

on \([a,b]\).

When the region between the curves is revolved around the \(x\)-axis,

\[ \boxed{ V = \int_a^b \pi\left([f(x)]^2-[g(x)]^2\right)\,dx } \]

Washer Method Example 1

Find the volume of the solid generated by revolving the region bounded above by

\[ f(x)=\sqrt{x} \]

and below by

\[ g(x)=\frac{1}{x} \]

over \([1,3]\) around the \(x\)-axis.

Rotation Around the y-Axis

When the region under \(f(x)\) is revolved around the \(y\)-axis, the volume is given by

\[ V=\pi\int_c^d [g(y)]^2\,dy \]

where \(x=g(y)\) is the inverse function of \(y=f(x)\) and \(y\) ranges from \(c\) to \(d\).

Rotation Around the y-Axis Example 1

Find the volume of the solid generated by revolving the region under the function \(f(x)=\frac{1}{x}\) around the \(y\)-axis for \(y\) in \([\frac{1}{2},1]\) using the method for rotation around the \(y\)-axis.

Function Plot

Washer Method with a Different Axis of Rotation

For a region revolved around a horizontal line \(y=c\):

\(V=\pi\int_a^b\left([R(x)]^2-[r(x)]^2\right)\,dx\)

where

\(R(x)=\) distance from \(y=c\) to the outer curve

\(r(x)=\) distance from \(y=c\) to the inner curve

If the region is bounded above by \(f(x)\) and below by \(g(x)\), then the radii depend on the location of the axis of rotation.

For rotation about \(y=c\) below the region:

\(R(x)=f(x)-c,\qquad r(x)=g(x)-c\)

For rotation about \(y=c\) above the region:

\(R(x)=c-g(x),\qquad r(x)=c-f(x)\)

Table of Antiderivatives: Trig

\[ \begin{aligned} \int \sec^2 x\,dx &= \tan x+C \\ \int \csc^2 x\,dx &= -\cot x+C \\ \int \sec x\tan x\,dx &= \sec x+C \\ \int \csc x\cot x\,dx &= -\csc x+C \\ \int \tan x\,dx &= \ln|\sec x|+C \\ \int \cot x\,dx &= \ln|\sin x|+C \\ \int \sec x\,dx &= \ln|\sec x+\tan x|+C \\ \int \csc x\,dx &= -\ln|\csc x+\cot x|+C \end{aligned} \]

Table of Antiderivatives: Other

\[ \begin{aligned} \int a^x\,dx &= \frac{a^x}{\ln a}+C,\quad a>0,\ a\neq1 \\ \int \log_a x\,dx &= x\log_a x-\frac{x}{\ln a}+C,\quad a>0,\ a\neq1 \\ \int \frac{1}{a^2+x^2}\,dx &= \frac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)+C \\ \int \frac{1}{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}\,dx &= \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)+C \\ \int \frac{1}{x\sqrt{x^2-a^2}}\,dx &= \frac{1}{a}\sec^{-1}\left|\frac{x}{a}\right|+C \\ \int \frac{1}{x^2-a^2}\,dx &= \frac{1}{2a}\ln\left|\frac{x-a}{x+a}\right|+C \end{aligned} \]

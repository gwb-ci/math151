Power Series and Functions

Course Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Define a power series and determine its radius and interval of convergence.
  • Represent rational functions as power series using the geometric series.
  • Construct new power series by combining and multiplying known power series.
  • Differentiate and integrate power series term by term.
  • Determine where a power series representation is valid.

Definition of a Power Series

A power series centered at \(a\) is an infinite series of the form

\[ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n=c_0+c_1(x-a)+c_2(x-a)^2+\cdots \]

where:

  • \(a\) is the center of the series.
  • \(c_n\) are the coefficients.
  • \(x\) is the variable.

Radius of Convergence

For a power series

\[ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n, \]

there is a radius of convergence \(R\) such that:

  • The series converges when \(|x-a|<R\).
  • The series diverges when \(|x-a|>R\).
  • Convergence at \(x=a-R\) and \(x=a+R\) must be checked separately.

Review: Interval of Convergence

The radius of convergence tells us how far from the center the series converges.

The interval of convergence tells us exactly which real numbers \(x\) produce a convergent series.

In for \(x\) in the interval of convergence, we can treat the powerseries as a function of \(x\).

Representing Functions as Power Series

  • A power series is an infinite polynomial.
  • Every power series has a radius of convergence.
  • A function can be represented by a power series where the series converges to that function.

How can we find power series representations of functions?

Use Geometric Series

A geometric series is a series of the form

\[ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}ar^n =a+ar+ar^2+ar^3+\cdots \]

  • If \(|r|<1\), the series converges, and

\[ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}ar^n=\frac{a}{1-r}. \]

Example 1

Rewrite as a power series and find the interval of convergence.

\[ f(x)=\frac{1}{1+x^3} \]

Example 2

Rewrite as a power series and find the interval of convergence.

\[ f(x)=\frac{x^2}{4-x^2} \]

Example 3

Rewrite as a power series and find the interval of convergence.

\[ f(x)=\frac{x^3}{2-x} \]

Combining Power Series

Suppose \(f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nx^n\) and \(g(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_nx^n\)

\[ f(x)+ g(x)= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(a_n+ b_n)x^n \]

and

\[ cf(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}ca_nx^n. \]

The are only valid in the interval of convergence.

Multipling and Substituting

Multiplication by a power of \(x\) gives

\[ x^mf(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nx^{n+m}. \]

We can also substitute another expression in place of \(x\).

\[ f(bx^m)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nb^nx^{mn}. \]

For the last expression, the new series is valid wherever \(bx^m\) lies in the original interval of convergence.

Differentiating Power Series

Suppose a power series represents a function \(f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n\) with radius of convergence \(R>0\).

We can differentiate the series term by term:

\[ f'(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} nc_n(x-a)^{n-1} \]

for \(|x-a|<R\).

The differentiated power series has the same radius of convergence.

Example 7: Differentiating a Power Series

Recall that

\[ \frac{1}{1-x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x^n, \qquad |x|<1. \]

Differentiate both sides to find a power series for

\[ f(x)=\frac{1}{(1-x)^2}. \]

Integrating Power Series

Suppose \(f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n\) has radius of convergence \(R>0\).

We can integrate term by term:

\[ \int f(x)\,dx = C+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{c_n}{n+1}(x-a)^{n+1} \]

for \(|x-a|<R\).

The integrated series has the same radius of convergence.

Example 8: Integrating a Power Series

We know that

\[ \frac{1}{1+x}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^nx^n, \qquad |x|<1. \]

Integrate both sides to find a power series representation for

\[ f(x)=\ln(1+x). \]

Example 9: An Inverse Tangent Series

Recall that

\[ \frac{1}{1+x^2} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^nx^{2n}, \qquad |x|<1. \]

Use integration to find a power series for

\[ f(x)=\arctan x. \]

Determine the radius and interval of convergence of the resulting series.

Uniqueness of Power Series

Suppose two power series centered at \(a\) represent the same function on an open interval containing \(a\):

\[ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n(x-a)^n = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_n(x-a)^n. \]

Then \(c_n=d_n\) for every \(n\).

Two power series are equal if and only if their coeffiecients are equal.

Multiplication of Power Series

Suppose

\(f(x)=a_0+a_1x+a_2x^2+\cdots\) and \(g(x)=b_0+b_1x+b_2x^2+\cdots\)

Their product is

\[ f(x)g(x)=c_0+c_1x+c_2x^2+\cdots, \]

where

\[ c_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_kb_{n-k}. \]
